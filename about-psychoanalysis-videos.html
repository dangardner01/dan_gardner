Explore Psychoanalysis Through Video

These curated video resources offer accessible, in-depth explanations of psychoanalytic theory and practice — designed for patients, families, clinicians, and anyone curious about how psychoanalytic psychotherapy works and what the research says about its effectiveness.

The series covers three core areas: what psychoanalysis actually is (and how it compares to other therapies), the most common myths and the evidence-based realities behind them, and the research on whether psychoanalytic treatment is effective. Together they offer a complete picture of a deeply thoughtful and scientifically supported approach to mental health.

Common Misconceptions

Psychoanalysis: Myths & Realities

Source: Washington Psychoanalytic Legislative Work Group

Myth

Treatment requires an unreasonable amount of time.

Reality

Extended therapy yields better long-term outcomes. Consumer Reports surveys of 4,000 patients found that longer treatment produced greater improvement and lasting results.

Myth

Therapy focuses entirely on the unchangeable past.

Reality

The approach explores present experiences while recognizing how history influences current situations and future possibilities. The goal is expanding future options, not dwelling on the past.

Myth

Psychoanalysis creates unhealthy dependence on the therapist.

Reality

Psychoanalytic practitioners actively help clients develop emotional autonomy. Dependent feelings are explored and worked through, not fostered. The goal is greater independence and self-understanding.

Myth

It is prohibitively expensive and only for the wealthy.

Reality

The long-term costs of untreated conditions — hospitalization, incarceration, chronic suffering — far exceed the cost of psychoanalytic treatment. Low-cost options are also available through training institutes and clinics.

Myth

Psychoanalysis is not supported by research.

Reality

There is substantial evidence in the research literature that psychoanalytic treatment works — including multiple meta-analyses showing effect sizes equivalent to or exceeding those of other evidence-based treatments.

Myth

It is only effective for the "worried well," not serious conditions.

Reality

Psychoanalytic therapy has proven effective for severe mental illnesses previously considered resistant to other interventions, including suicidality, PTSD, eating disorders, borderline personality disorder, and chronic depression.
